# Visual Design Direction

**Product:** Music as Medicine  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Early direction captured

## Purpose

This document records the initial visual and interaction direction for Music as Medicine. It is intentionally early and will be refined after the screen-by-screen experience and prototype structure are established.

## Reference materials

- `microdosing-magic-music-mix_cover.png`
- `microdosing-magic-music-mix.pdf`

The reference guide is visual and tonal inspiration only. Its content, sequence, and instructions are not automatically part of the app experience.

## Brand relationship

- **Music as Medicine** remains the working app name.
- **Microdosing Magic Music Mix** may be used as the name of the free experience, but it is not required.
- If the free-experience name creates confusion or weakens the connection to Music as Medicine, a clearer name can be developed during messaging design.

## Core aesthetic

The direction is retro analog editorial, drawing from the 1980s and 1990s without becoming neon synthwave, arcade-themed, or a literal reproduction of an old operating system.

The experience should combine:

- Editorial poster composition
- Analog music culture
- Geometric typography
- Warm paper-like color fields
- Bold, simple graphic shapes
- Tactile print and halftone details
- Generous negative space
- Playful interface moments
- Calm, legible journaling surfaces

## Desired feeling

The interface should feel:

- Playful
- Expressive
- Warm
- Creative
- Nostalgic
- Human
- Spacious
- Intentional
- Inviting rather than clinical
- Retro without becoming costume-like

## Typography

### Primary typefaces

- **Montserrat Classic**
- **Neue Einstellung**

### Proposed roles

- Use Montserrat Classic for large titles, important actions, and bold editorial statements.
- Use Neue Einstellung for body copy, prompts, labels, and quieter supporting text.
- Use widely tracked lowercase text for small labels, section markers, playlist metadata, and navigational details.
- Use italic sparingly for presenter-style notes, asides, or gentle invitations.

### Typography principles

- Prefer lowercase headings when appropriate.
- Use strong scale contrast between display type and supporting text.
- Preserve generous line spacing in journal and coaching content.
- Avoid using extreme letter spacing for long paragraphs.
- Keep form labels and calendar information highly legible.
- Verify webfont files and web-use permissions before implementation.

## Color direction

The reference palette begins with:

- Butter yellow
- Near-black ink
- Warm white
- Raspberry or magenta
- Deep aubergine

The app can introduce additional retro colors rather than remaining limited to the original cover.

### Provisional supporting colors

- Dusty teal
- Cobalt or cassette-label blue
- Warm coral
- Burnt orange
- Soft mint
- Lavender or muted violet

### Color-use principles

- Keep warm yellow, ink, white, and berry as the recognizable foundation.
- Use supporting colors in purposeful blocks, tags, calendar entries, feeling families, and playlist roles.
- Avoid using every accent color on a single screen.
- Use white or warm-white journaling surfaces for longer reading and writing.
- Maintain accessible text contrast even when using period-inspired colors.
- Do not depend on color alone to communicate calendar or feeling categories.

## Imagery and graphic language

The visual library can draw from analog music culture broadly, including:

- Cassette tapes
- Headphones
- Vinyl records
- Radios and portable stereos
- Speakers
- Equalizers
- Tape decks
- Mixers
- Microphones
- Analog meters
- Handwritten track lists
- Ticket stubs and concert ephemera
- Record-store stickers and labels

Imagery can combine clean cutouts, silhouettes, line drawings, halftone texture, and cropped photography.

## Interface expression

The chosen direction is editorial design with playful analog controls.

### Editorial foundation

- Poster-like screen compositions
- Bold type hierarchy
- Structured blocks of color
- Asymmetrical layouts where they do not harm usability
- Worksheet-inspired lines and fields
- Large moments of negative space

### Analog interaction details

- Track-number labels
- Side A and Side B language when meaningful
- Tape-counter or equalizer-inspired progress
- Cassette-label cards for songs and playlists
- Tactile-looking play, pause, save, and reorder controls
- Subtle reel or equalizer movement
- Calendar markers that feel like stickers, labels, or recorded-session notes

These details should add delight without making core actions difficult to understand.

## Application areas

### Landing experience

- Strong poster composition
- One central analog object or collage
- Oversized title
- Short promise
- Clear modern call to action

### Coaching and journaling

- Warm-white or pale-color writing surfaces
- One prompt at a time
- Large readable type
- Underlines, ruled-paper cues, and label-maker details
- Minimal visual movement while writing

### Song selection

- Track cards resembling cassette labels, record sleeves, or handwritten mix lists
- Clear artist and title hierarchy
- Feeling and playlist-role tags
- Analog imagery without depending on copyrighted album artwork

### Feeling Map

- A visual composition assembled from the user's confirmed words
- Layered labels, connecting lines, color blocks, and track references
- A personal artifact rather than a technical report

### Rhythm and calendar

- A modern calendar with analog-style markers
- Distinct colors and icons for listening, meditation, reflection, and intention practices
- Entries that open directly into the relevant ritual or content
- Rhythm expressed through repeated visual motifs, not just dates in a grid

### Results and export

- A collectible mixtape-insert or zine-like artifact
- Strong printable hierarchy
- Personal title, track list, Feeling Map, and ritual
- Visual continuity between the app and downloaded PDF

## Motion direction

Motion should be subtle and purposeful:

- Slow cassette-reel movement during audio
- Gentle equalizer response
- Track-counter changes during progress
- Soft slide or flip transitions between journey stages
- Small tactile feedback when placing songs or calendar markers

Avoid constant animation, flashing neon, aggressive glitch effects, or motion that competes with reflection.

## Accessibility and usability guardrails

- Retro styling must not reduce legibility.
- Body copy should use comfortable sizes and line lengths.
- All audio requires transcripts.
- Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Controls need recognizable labels in addition to analog styling.
- Calendar categories require text or icons as well as color.
- Journal fields must work with keyboard, screen reader, and mobile input.

## Reference-guide observations

The seven-page guide reinforces several useful patterns:

- A warm yellow background can carry both playfulness and calm.
- Deep aubergine and berry create richer contrast than black alone.
- Split color panels can distinguish preparation, examples, and user action.
- Large lowercase headlines feel approachable and editorial.
- Worksheet lines translate naturally into guided app inputs.
- Sparse closing pages create emotional space.
- The overall voice supports play, movement, experimentation, and enjoyment.

These observations inform the visual system only. They do not determine the product's final content or flow.

## Decisions confirmed

- Use a retro 1980s-1990s direction.
- Favor analog editorial design over neon synthwave.
- Use Montserrat Classic and Neue Einstellung.
- Expand beyond the original yellow, black, white, and berry palette.
- Draw imagery from the full world of analog music, not cassette tapes alone.
- Use editorial design with playful analog controls rather than a fully skeuomorphic player.
- Treat the attached guide as reference only.
